# The baryonic-to-halo mass relation in ΛCDM cosmology: a two-branch law from the energy cascade of dark matter, tested with SPARC

Xu Z., 2026, *The baryonic-to-halo mass relation in ΛCDM cosmology: a two-branch law from the energy cascade of dark matter, tested with SPARC*, Zenodo [doi:10.5281/zenodo.24094605](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24094605); the earlier version is hosted on arXiv as [arXiv:2203.06899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.06899).

## Major findings

- Dark matter flow carries kinetic energy from small to large mass scales at a rate ε<sub>u</sub> = −(3/2)u²/t ≈ −4.6×10⁻⁷ m²s⁻³, where u is the velocity dispersion of all particles.
- If baryons and dark matter in a halo share this rate and the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation v<sub>f</sub><sup>4</sup> = Gm<sub>b</sub>a<sub>0</sub> holds, small haloes give m<sub>b</sub> ∝ m<sub>h</sub><sup>4/3</sup> with v<sub>f</sub> ∝ v<sub>cir</sub>, and large haloes give m<sub>b</sub> ∝ m<sub>h</sub><sup>4/9</sup> with v<sub>f</sub> ∝ v<sub>cir</sub><sup>1/3</sup>.
- The two branches meet at m<sub>hc</sub> ≈ 1.34×10¹² M☉, where the baryon-to-halo mass ratio peaks at A ≈ 0.076.
- On the 123 SPARC galaxies with a measured flat rotation velocity, the data require a break (ΔBIC = 72 over a single power law) at M<sub>200</sub> = 10<sup>11.95</sup> M☉ and V<sub>200</sub> = 147 km s⁻¹, against the predicted 10<sup>12.12</sup> M☉ and 143 km s⁻¹.
- Above the break the slope is 0.21 (68 per cent range −0.04 to 0.34; predicted 0.44), and below it the slope is 1.55 (1.44 to 1.62), steeper than the predicted 4/3.
- Fits without the abundance-matching prior, restricted to galaxies with well-measured halo masses, keep the break (10<sup>12.02</sup> M☉) and are consistent with both predicted slopes.
- Averaged over the halo mass function, the mean baryon-to-halo mass ratio is 0.024, and the relation puts 7.6 per cent of all baryons in galaxies if only haloes above 10<sup>12</sup> M☉ host them, rising to 12 per cent if haloes down to 10<sup>10</sup> M☉ do.
